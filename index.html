02 — What it costs

The information is not missing. Nothing decides what it means.

  1. The public record

    Every one of these is true. Not one of them was written to explain who you are now.

  2. Many fragments

    None of those versions is false. None is complete, current, or ordered by importance — because no one ordered them at all.

  3. Different readers, different fragments

    A prospective client. A patient. A board considering you for a seat. A journalist checking a name before a call. A conference organiser. An investor. Someone deciding whether to make the referral.

  4. No clear source of truth

    They all do the same thing first, and none of them tell you they did it. A search engine does not rank the truth. It ranks what has a page.

You spent a career earning a reputation. The internet is assembling it without you.

I found this problem by reading about people, not by building websites.

I came across accomplished people while researching them through press releases and public records. The same thing kept appearing: a real professional reputation spread across press releases, directories, company pages, social profiles and other people’s websites, with no clear home around the name.

I started building those homes because the problem was obvious once I had seen it. The person had already done the work. The internet simply was not presenting it as one thing.

No award, title, date or quote appears on a site unless you have confirmed it. If it cannot be verified it is marked for review, not filled in.
